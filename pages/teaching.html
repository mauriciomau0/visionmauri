How I teach

The methods I rely on in the classroom:

  • Problem-based learning: classes start from a question worth answering.
  • Live coding: models are built in front of the class, errors included, so the debugging is part of the lesson.
  • Scaffolding: support is heavy at the start of a topic and deliberately withdrawn as students take it over.
  • Active recall: students retrieve concepts rather than reread them, which is what makes them stick.
  • Formative assessment: frequent low-stakes checks that tell me what to reteach while there is still time to reteach it.

My classrooms are never homogeneous — people show up from healthcare, finance, security, engineering, all with a totally different relationship to math and code. So I start from a real problem instead of a formula. Everyone gets to walk in through their own door, and scaffolding plus feedback along the way gets them to roughly the same place, even though they started from opposite ends of the room. Underneath that is the bigger thing I keep circling back to: work used to be how you built an identity, made progress, earned your place. Now AI writes, calculates, and designs faster than any of us, and that whole arrangement stops making sense. What looks like laziness is usually someone who can't see the point anymore. What still works is a real problem worth wrestling with, a question they actually want the answer to, and someone noticing they tried. I'm not trying to out-run AI. I just want a room that people still have a reason to walk into.
